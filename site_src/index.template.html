The long-term thesis · five years, to 2031

    
NEAR is turning from a chain people use directly into a settlement layer for cross-chain money, more and more of it private. Its long-term value depends on getting paid for that flow, and on holders being able to check that it does.

    
In a year, Intents volume rose about {{volume_year}} times while active accounts fell {{accounts_year}}. For that to be worth owning, three things must be true by 2031:

    

      
Volume keeps growing
It must grow {{needed_floor}} to {{needed_cap}} a year for revenue to pay for all new NEAR. The recent trend is {{observed_growth}}.

      
NEAR gets paid
The take must hold near {{late_bps_after}} bps. If private swaps pay nothing it falls toward {{public_floor}} bps, and growth must reach {{needed_public}} a year.

      
Holders can check
Private totals must be open to audit, and it must be clear who holds the key. Today neither is true.

NEAR's revenue now comes out of the dark

What this shows

      
The first payment from the treasury came on {{first_private_text}}, the week Confidential Intents opened to all developers. The public part of revenue then stayed flat while the private part grew on top of it. Reported gross fees rose far less, from {{fee_bps_before}} to {{fee_bps_after}} bps, so NEAR's share of fees went from {{capture_before}} to {{capture_after}}.

      
What it breaks

      
Wallet receipts still match DefiLlama, within {{wallet_gap}}. But that check now proves only that money arrived. Nothing public links a private swap to a revenue payment. From outside, a payment out of the treasury looks the same whatever earned it. We have no evidence that it is anything other than fees. Nobody outside can show that it is.

      
What we cannot rule out

      
The route is not the source. Fees earned inside the private chain, a partner deal paid through a shielded account, or public fees held in shielded form would all look the same from outside. Revenue per dollar has spiked before, too: it reached {{early_peak_bps}} bps in {{early_peak_week}}, then fell back. This was the best thing to happen to NEAR's value capture this year, and nobody outside can show why it happened.

    

  

  

    
03 · The swaps

    
On 27 October the swaps moved too, and the take slipped

    

      
Until then, private swaps at the boundary were few. Then they multiplied about {{swaps_multiple}} times in days, and money entering the treasury went from {{flow_share_before}} of reported volume to {{flow_share_recent}}. If private swaps paid NEAR like public ones, revenue should have held. So far it has not: {{late_bps_before}} bps in the 30 days before, {{late_bps_after}} bps in the {{late_days}} days since, {{late_change}}. The API terms say the private fee is kept by a private company for its own account, so this is the leak we set out to look for.

      
It is too early to call. But it is a clean test, and it is running now. If private swaps pay NEAR, revenue per dollar holds. If they do not, it falls toward the part with no private trace, about {{public_floor}} bps. {{fit_sentence}}

08 · For NEAR

    
The fix is small, and it makes NEAR easier to own

    
Privacy for users and accounts for holders do not conflict. A daily total reveals nobody's trades. We drafted the proposal and built the parts anyone can use today.

    

      
Publish the totals
Each day: private volume, private fees, and the share sent to NEAR's wallets, signed and open to an auditor's check. Read the draft proposal.

      
Say who holds the key
Publish how the treasury key is held and who can use it, and the validator set of the private chain.

      
Put the rule on chain
State what share of the private fee reaches NEAR's wallets, and keep it where holders can see a change.

    

    

      
A weigh station anyone can call
{{contract_status}} reads the treasury straight from intents.near, with no oracle, and warns on chain if a key changes or money leaves fast. Any contract can read it.

      
An open daily feed
Every series here is free to use, with no key: weight, revenue source, flows and everything.

      
A tool for agents
A small server gives AI agents the same numbers, and a live weighing, through the Model Context Protocol. See it.

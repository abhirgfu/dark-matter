02 · The revenue

    
NEAR's revenue now comes out of the dark, and there is more of it

    

      
Revenue reaches NEAR's three wallets as withdrawals from intents.near. We checked {{payments_checked}} payments against transfers out of the private treasury, to the exact yoctoNEAR. A growing share start as a transfer out of the treasury. In our hand trace, all {{trace_treasury}} such payments came through {{trace_accounts}} different accounts, each used to pass the money on.

    

    

      
What NEAR keeps from each dollar of volume

      
Weekly revenue-wallet receipts divided by reported volume, by the route the money took

      

      
{{provenance_sentence}}

      

      
The query
{{query_provenance}}

    

    

      
What this settles

      
We started out asking whether private swaps pay NEAR nothing, because the API terms say a private company keeps the private fee for its own account. We found the opposite. The first payment from the treasury came on {{first_private_text}}, the week Confidential Intents opened to all developers. The public part of revenue then stayed flat while the private part grew on top of it. Reported gross fees rose far less, from {{fee_bps_before}} to {{fee_bps_after}} bps, so NEAR's share of fees went from {{capture_before}} to {{capture_after}}.

      
What it breaks

      
Wallet receipts still match DefiLlama, within {{wallet_gap}}. But that check now proves only that money arrived. Nothing public links a private swap to a revenue payment. From outside, a payment out of the treasury looks the same whatever earned it. We have no evidence that it is anything other than fees. Nobody outside can show that it is.

      
What we cannot rule out

      
The route is not the source. A new partner deal paid through a shielded account would look the same as fees from private swaps. And revenue per dollar has spiked before: it reached {{early_peak_bps}} bps in {{early_peak_week}}, then fell back. This is the best thing to happen to NEAR's value capture this year, and nobody outside can show why it happened. That is why the proposal below asks for the one number that would settle it.

08 · For NEAR

    
The fix is small, and it makes NEAR easier to own

    
Privacy for users and accounts for holders do not conflict. A daily total reveals nobody's trades. We drafted the proposal and built the parts anyone can use today.

    

      
Publish the totals
Each day: private volume, private fees, and the share sent to NEAR's wallets, signed and open to an auditor's check. Read the draft proposal.

      
Say who holds the key
Publish how the treasury key is held and who can use it, and the validator set of the private chain.

      
Put the rule on chain
State what share of the private fee reaches NEAR's wallets, and keep it where holders can see a change.

    

    

      
A weigh station anyone can call
{{contract_status}} reads the treasury straight from intents.near, with no oracle, and warns on chain if a key changes or money leaves fast. Any contract can read it.

      
An open daily feed
Every series here is free to use, with no key: weight, revenue source, flows and everything.

      
A tool for agents
A small server gives AI agents the same numbers, and a live weighing, through the Model Context Protocol. See it.

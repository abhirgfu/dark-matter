01 · The weight

    
The private economy weighs about $141.3M, and the public chain can prove it

    

      
Shielded balances live on a private chain with no public RPC or explorer. The tokens backing them do not. All of them sit under one account on intents.near, on the public chain, and anyone can read that balance at any past block.

    

    

      
Treasury value, daily

      
Our reading at the first block of each UTC day, in USD, against NEAR's published series

      

      
Built only from chain balances and public prices, our figure tracks NEAR's with a correlation of 0.997 over 246 days, and a median gap of 1.1%. It has read low since October, by 5.1% over the last 60 days, because 33 of 193 tokens have no public price history. Live prices close that gap: see the figure at the top.

      

      
The query
POST https://archival-rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com
{"method":"query","params":{"request_type":"call_function","block_id":<first block of the day>,
 "account_id":"intents.near","method_name":"mt_tokens_for_owner",
 "args_base64":<{"account_id":"51e8f94d...7cbe"}>}}

then mt_batch_balance_of for those tokens at the same block.
Value = sum(balance / 10^decimals * DefiLlama daily price).

02 · The revenue

    
NEAR's revenue now comes out of the dark

    

      
Revenue reaches NEAR's three wallets as withdrawals from intents.near. We checked 5,083 payments against transfers out of the private treasury, to the exact yoctoNEAR. A growing share start as a transfer out of the treasury. In our hand trace, all 33 such payments came through 33 different accounts, each used to pass the money on.

    

    

      
What NEAR keeps from each dollar of volume

      
Weekly revenue-wallet receipts divided by reported volume, by the route the money took

      

      
In the 60 days before 21 September, NEAR kept 2.83 bps of volume. Since then it has kept 5.06 bps. The part with no private trace barely moved, from 1.45 to 1.39 bps. All of the gain left the treasury first: 1.38 to 3.67 bps.

      

      
The query
1. Payments: every native NEAR transfer from intents.near into
   buybacks.multisignature.near, fefundsadmin.sputnik-dao.near, 1csfundsadmin.sputnik-dao.near
   POST https://transfers.main.fastnear.com/v0/transfers
   {"account_id":<wallet>,"direction":"receiver"}

2. Treasury outflows: every wNEAR transfer out of the treasury
   {"account_id":"51e8f94d...7cbe","direction":"sender",
    "asset_id":"nep245:intents.near:nep141:wrap.near"}

3. A payment is private when an outflow of exactly the same yoctoNEAR left the treasury in the
   six hours before it. Each outflow can explain one payment.

4. Volume: https://api.llama.fi/summary/dexs/near-intents
   bps = weekly receipts / weekly volume * 10,000

Code: pipeline/metrics.py match_payments, pipeline/analyze.py revenue

    

    

      
What this shows

      
The first payment from the treasury came on 7 July, the week Confidential Intents opened to all developers. The public part of revenue then stayed flat while the private part grew on top of it. Reported gross fees rose far less, from 13.9 to 15.9 bps, so NEAR's share of fees went from 20% to 32%.

      
What it breaks

      
Wallet receipts still match DefiLlama, within 1.0%. But that check now proves only that money arrived. Nothing public links a private swap to a revenue payment. From outside, a payment out of the treasury looks the same whatever earned it. We have no evidence that it is anything other than fees. Nobody outside can show that it is.

      
What we cannot rule out

      
The route is not the source. Fees earned inside the private chain, a partner deal paid through a shielded account, or public fees held in shielded form would all look the same from outside. Revenue per dollar has spiked before, too: it reached 4.2 bps in June, then fell back. This was the best thing to happen to NEAR's value capture this year, and nobody outside can show why it happened.

    

  

  

    
03 · The swaps

    
On 27 October the swaps moved too, and the take slipped

    

      
Until then, private swaps at the boundary were few. Then they multiplied about 29 times in days. If private swaps paid NEAR like public ones, revenue should have held. So far it has not: 5.32 bps in the 30 days before, 4.56 bps in the 11 days since, −14%. The API terms say the private fee is kept by a private company for its own account, so this is the leak we set out to look for.

      
It is too early to call. But it is a clean test, and it is running now. If private swaps pay NEAR, revenue per dollar holds. If they do not, it falls toward the part with no private trace, about 1.26 bps.

    

    

      
Private swaps seen at the boundary

      
Accounts entering and leaving the treasury within the hour, per hour, in sampled hours

      

      
In 3 sampled hours before 27 October we saw about 18 private swaps an hour. In 7.7 hours since, about 519. These are only the swaps we can pair, so the true counts are higher.

The fee nobody can see leaves a fingerprint

      
Private swaps between different assets since 27 October, by what was lost between entry and exit, in basis points

      

      
These 1,594 swaps do not spread smoothly. They bunch near 19, 77 and 146 bps. That looks like a price list, and no public document gives one. The documented public default is 20 bps. Stablecoin swaps cost a median 1.9 bps, close to the documented 1 bp. At the sampled rate, private swappers lose about $223.1K a day between entry and exit, against $60.2K a day reaching NEAR's wallets from every source. Part of that gap is solver spread and price movement. How much is fee, and who keeps it, cannot be seen.

      

      
The query
Every transfer of any size, both ways, for sampled hours.
A pair is an account that is not a solver, with exactly one transfer in and one out, inside the hour.
cost = (usd_in - usd_out) / usd_in * 10,000
Code: pipeline/fetch_flows.py windows, pipeline/metrics.py pair_accounts

08 · For NEAR

    
The fix is small, and it makes NEAR easier to own

    
Privacy for users and accounts for holders do not conflict. A daily total reveals nobody's trades. We drafted the proposal and built the parts anyone can use today.

    

      
Publish the totals
Each day: private volume, private fees, and the share sent to NEAR's wallets, signed and open to an auditor's check. Read the draft proposal.

      
Say who holds the key
Publish how the treasury key is held and who can use it, and the validator set of the private chain.

      
Put the rule on chain
State what share of the private fee reaches NEAR's wallets, and keep it where holders can see a change.

    

    

      
A weigh station anyone can call
dark-matter reads the treasury straight from intents.near, with no oracle, and warns on chain if a key changes or money leaves fast. Any contract can read it.

      
An open daily feed
Every series here is free to use, with no key: weight, revenue source, flows and everything.

      
A tool for agents
A small server gives AI agents the same numbers, and a live weighing, through the Model Context Protocol. See it.

    

  


  

    
09 · Limits

    
What we cannot see

    

      
  • We see the boundary, not the inside. Swaps on the private chain, their fees and their solvers cannot be observed.
  • Paired accounts show cost only for accounts that enter and leave inside the sampled hour. A swap that returns to another account is missed.
  • The revenue match is exact to the yoctoNEAR, so it can only undercount. A sweep that merges two transfers looks public.
  • A payment out of the treasury shows where money came from, not what earned it.
  • Tokens with no public price history are left unpriced, not guessed. That is why our daily series reads below NEAR's.
  • The ranges used to find the fee's price points were chosen after seeing the data.
  • Swaps made inside a shielded balance never cross the boundary, so we cannot count them. The low swap count before 27 October shows only that few swaps crossed.
  • The daily flow census covers transfers of $2,500 or more, about 95% of dollars by a sample.
  • Eleven days since 27 October is short. Revenue per dollar can move for many reasons, including mix, price and the timing of sweeps.
  • The 1Click API terms limit how long API data may be kept, so no quote or Explorer data is stored here.
